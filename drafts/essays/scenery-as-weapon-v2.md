# Scenery as Weapon

*How aesthetic order and administrative classification erase political life*

A scenery is never only a view. It is a decision about what belongs inside the frame.

Land can be shown as an uninterrupted horizon while the labour that sustains it disappears. A settlement can be represented as an obstruction while its histories of residence go unspoken. The image need not lie about what is visible. Its authority can depend on making everything outside the frame seem irrelevant.

The concept note for *“…ing: Sceneries Without Sovereignty”* locates sovereignty in relations of shared responsibility, collective care, and continuity. It asks what happens when land becomes scenery and language is severed from the lives that give it meaning.[^concept] I approach that question through two apparently distant archives: electoral enumeration in West Bengal and film-certification records. Each produces a surface that others encounter as settled: a roll of recognised electors; a film permitted to circulate. Behind those surfaces lie decisions, classifications, and opportunities to contest them.

The comparison has limits. An absent electoral entry and a film modification do not have the same consequences. Nor does a shared vocabulary of deletion establish a common cause. What brings them into this essay is a question about representation: what work disappears when we encounter only the authorised result?

## 1. From landscape to legibility

An electoral roll gives administrative form to a population. It also places demands on people: names must be recognisable, addresses locatable, and relationships translatable into the categories of a form. The record can help secure recognition. It can also become the ground on which recognition has to be defended.

My West Bengal archive now contains a completed extraction that the earlier version of this essay could only propose. The repository brings together two distinct projects: collecting the 2002 electoral-roll PDFs and extracting the 2025 ASD/SIR reports. The latter concern enumeration forms described in the source documents as “uncollectable.” The official website identifies the associated lists as people present in the 2025 rolls but absent from the draft roll for the Special Intensive Revision of 2026.[^wb][^asd] The year of the source and the year of the revision must both remain visible.

The completed extraction, documented in August 2026, contains 5,819,543 elector records from 80,651 readable PDFs. Its inventory accounts for 80,655 files across 24 source district directories and 294 assembly constituencies; four files are empty. A fresh pass through the entire cleaned dataset for this essay confirmed the row total, complete EPIC fields, unique document-and-serial keys, and valid analysis categories. The cleaning process preserves a route back to each record's PDF, page, table, and row.[^lineage] These are findings about the construction of the dataset. They do not establish the correctness of every administrative decision recorded within it.

That distinction changes what the essay can say. There is now a substantial archive to examine, rather than an imagined future extraction. But 5,819,543 extracted records cannot simply become 5,819,543 people finally disenfranchised. Draft omission, an administrative reason, a decision after a claim, and presence or absence in a later roll are different observations. The official SIR portal separately lists the final roll published on 28 February 2026 and subsequent supplementary and deletion lists. Those later stages have not been linked to this extraction for this essay.[^sir]

The 2002 material remains a separate historical source. Reading it alongside the newer reports requires work on changed boundaries, polling parts, identifiers, and the uncertainty of matching records across time. A name that cannot be matched is not, by that fact alone, evidence of exclusion. The promise of the archive lies in making such questions investigable, not in answering them before the comparison has been made.

## 2. Deletion as method

The administrative word at the centre of this dataset is worth lingering over: *uncollectable*. It describes the outcome of an attempt to obtain a form. Read too quickly, it can become a description of the person from whom the form was sought. A failed encounter becomes a stable attribute.

The reason categories retain traces of different encounters. The Bengali label *মৃত* is mapped to `death`; *স্থায়ীভাবে স্থানান্তরিত* to `permanently_shifted`; *খুঁজে পাওয়া যায়নি / অনুপস্থিত* to `untraceable_or_absent`; and *ইতিমধ্যে তালিকাভুক্ত* to `already_enrolled`. Corresponding English and Devanagari labels enter the same documented mapping.[^mapping] These are source classifications, not independently established biographies. In particular, the combined category “untraceable or absent” does not tell us which condition applied, for how long, or what happened afterwards.

Translation here is part of the argument. A canonical code makes comparison possible, but it can erase a distinction if its apparent precision exceeds that of the source. Keeping the Bengali label beside the derived category allows a reader to question the translation. Keeping the source coordinates allows that question to travel back to the document. Neither practice is merely technical housekeeping. Both preserve the possibility of disagreement.

The full-file count gives those distinctions scale: 2,416,131 records are classified as death; 1,987,636 as permanently shifted; 1,277,521 as untraceable or absent; and 138,255 as already enrolled.[^verified] The largest category accounts for 41.52 percent of extracted records. More than a million entries fall within the combined category that cannot distinguish being untraceable from being absent. The numbers show the composition of the recorded reasons. They do not verify the reasons, tell us whose claims succeeded, or measure any community's risk of exclusion.

The official press note of 16 December 2025 reported 76,637,529 electors and 70,816,630 collected enumeration forms. Their difference is 5,820,899, which exceeds the extracted record count by 1,356.[^press] That is an unresolved reconciliation difference, not a count of hidden deletions. The four empty PDFs cannot be assigned those missing records without evidence. A nearly matching total is useful precisely because it leaves a specific question to investigate.

The most concrete scene in this archive is a damaged name cell. The published audit identifies 6,979 missing cells across elector and related-person names, affecting 3,874 records in 3,092 documents. Of those cells, 6,924 contain only the font's undefined character, CID 0; another 55 are blank in the source. The missing fields include 3,435 elector names and 3,544 related-person names.[^names] These totals must not be turned into “6,979 missing voters.” A record can have both name fields affected, and the record itself survives.

According to the source audit, the undefined glyphs contain neither the original character identities nor distinct letter shapes from which OCR could recover them. The obstacle is therefore not simply a machine that has yet to learn Bengali. These particular PDFs no longer provide the information needed to reconstruct those names. The identifiers and document coordinates remain available for an authoritative cross-reference; the names must not be guessed.[^lineage]

A square on a page does not prove an intention to erase. It does reveal a limit in the record on which recognition may depend. This is where an argument about administrative power can become exact: the dataset can preserve the fact of damage without inventing either the missing name or the motive behind its loss.

To move from this archive to a claim about unequal exclusion would require further evidence. Counts by locality need comparable pre-revision electorate denominators. Claims about final outcomes need later rolls and decisions. Accounts of the burden of contesting an omission need testimony. A surname should not become a shortcut for assigning someone's religion or caste. The question is how the process distributes recognition and the labour of securing it; the answer must be earned at the scale at which it is asserted.

## 3. Cultural governance and the censor's cut

A certified film offers another apparently complete surface. Its continuity can conceal the labour through which it became acceptable for circulation. A muted word may leave a perceptible gap. A removed scene may leave no trace that a viewer can identify without access to another version or a modification record.

The censorboard materials in this project make that process available for study. The repository holds saved aggregates attributed to certification metadata and modification records, alongside visual drafts. Their vocabulary includes deletion, replacement, and insertion; selected description tokens include “muted,” “word,” and “visuals.” These are traces of instructions about a film. They must not automatically be read as the words spoken in it.[^cbfc]

The distinction matters aesthetically as well as statistically. A count of the word “muted” tells us about how an intervention is described. It does not tell us which voice was muted, what the sentence meant, or how the alteration changed the film. To recover those relations requires close reading of individual instructions and, where available, the work to which they refer. Otherwise the researcher repeats the compression of the administrative record: an act with a context becomes a token with a frequency.

The saved figures also show why a political argument needs a demanding method. An earlier caption described a rise in recorded modified duration per certificate between 2020 and 2021 as regulatory hardening. The aggregate alone cannot establish that explanation. Changes in archive coverage, the kinds of films represented, recording practices, or the denominator could also affect the comparison. Likewise, differences between language categories cannot on their own demonstrate discriminatory treatment. The underlying release files and the exact historical aggregation have not been independently reproduced for this revision, so those numerical comparisons remain outside the essay's evidentiary claims.[^audit]

What remains is a sharper question about the finished surface. The roll and the certified film each arrive with institutional authority, while the operations that produced them require additional work to inspect. Their relationship is not that a person and a scene are interchangeable objects. It is that public visibility can depend on decisions whose history is absent from the object presented to us.

An archive of modifications can open that history. It can show where to look for an instruction, a justification, a negotiation, or a refusal. Its political value grows when it returns us to those particulars. It diminishes when a graph announces a motive that the records have not established.

## 4. Against innocent data

Rebuilding an archive does not put the researcher outside the politics of classification. Every normalization, join, translation, and graphic introduces another decision about what will remain visible. A clean table can make a damaged source look complete. A map can make an unevenly collected archive look like a continuous territory.

The West Bengal extraction offers a more accountable practice: preserve a row even when a name is damaged; keep the source category beside the analysis category; record an implausible value rather than silently making it plausible. Its documentation treats uncertainty as something to locate. A reader can ask which cell failed, how it failed, and what kind of evidence would be needed to repair it.

This does not make the dataset innocent. It makes parts of its construction answerable. Structural checks can establish that extracted rows have identifiers and valid category codes. They cannot establish that an official was right to classify a person as absent, or that every eligible person could navigate a claims process. The quality of extraction and the justice of administration are separate questions, and both deserve scrutiny.

The same obligation applies to a visual. A map of raw counts should not pass as a map of exclusion rates. An unreadable PDF should not become a locality with zero affected electors. A chart of selected action-content combinations should not claim to describe all interventions. A caption is part of the evidence: it tells the reader what population, period, and unit the marks stand for.

There is also a difference between making power inspectable and making people unnecessarily exposed. This essay needs aggregate findings and a traceable method, not a public display of names, relatives, and electoral identifiers. Retaining those fields for controlled verification does not require reproducing them in the publication. An archive built to question administrative authority should remain attentive to the authority it acquires over the people it describes.

## 5. Scenery without sovereignty

Scenery begins to serve power when the frame is mistaken for the whole world. The landscape appears without the relations that sustain it. The roll appears without the encounters and disputes through which recognition was determined. The film appears without the instructions that shaped its public form.

These absences are not identical. Keeping their differences visible is what makes comparison useful. A damaged name cell is a source defect; a draft omission is an administrative status; an unjust exclusion is a substantive finding that needs evidence. If the essay collapses them into one figure of disappearance, it reproduces the flattening it seeks to oppose.

The concept note's account of sovereignty asks us to begin elsewhere: with shared care, continuity, and the practices through which people sustain a world together. An electoral entry cannot contain all those relations. Yet the ability to secure and contest an entry can matter profoundly within them. A film certificate cannot exhaust a language's life. Yet decisions about what may circulate shape the conditions under which that life is heard.

The work of this archive is modest in form and consequential in possibility. It keeps a route back from a number to a document. It refuses to fill a damaged name with a guess. It leaves a translation open to challenge. It distinguishes a published finding from a question that remains unanswered. These are ways of preventing administrative finality from becoming intellectual finality.

To write against scenery is to keep asking what the view has required us to forget, and who can still contest its terms. The task is not to recover a pure view. It is to break the view's authority.
