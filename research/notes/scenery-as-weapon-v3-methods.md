## Terms

- **SIR:** Special Intensive Revision of the electoral roll. Here the source reports concern 2025 enumeration for the SIR 2026 draft.
- **ASD:** absent, shifted, or dead. Official usage also includes dead/duplicate under the last letter, or uses ASDD. The four analysis categories are retained explicitly rather than inferred from the acronym. The [Election Commission press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2201588&lang=1&reg=3) describes absent, shifted, or dead/duplicate lists.
- **EPIC:** Electors Photo Identity Card; the source records retain its identifier. The manuscript says “electoral identifier” and does not introduce the acronym.
- **OCR:** optical character recognition. The manuscript explains the relevant limit in ordinary language; this acronym is omitted from the essay.
- **CID 0:** character identifier zero, mapped to the font's undefined `.notdef` glyph in the audited source cells. “Undefined character” and “square” in the essay refer to this diagnosed condition. OCR cannot reconstruct letters where neither their identities nor distinct shapes survive in the source.
- **Recognition:** in this essay, administrative acceptance as an elector attached to a place. It does not mean citizenship adjudication, property ownership, or the whole of social belonging.

## Claim boundaries and choices

The single limits paragraph distinguishes extracted records and draft omissions from final disenfranchisement, verified reasons, targeting, and intentional glyph damage. Subsequent paragraphs make an interpretive argument about categories, residence, the work of seeking inclusion, and the appearance of a finished record.

The practical burden claim is grounded in section 5 of the December 16 press note: a person absent from the draft is directed to submit Form 6 with Annexure IV and other documents. The essay also acknowledges the same note's official assistance and camps. This is not a claim about the legal burden of proof in every proceeding or a current guide to electoral remedies.

The residence discussion does not infer that the 1,987,636 permanently-shifted records concern eviction or forced migration. Work, family, loss of housing, and voluntary movement are presented as possible circumstances that this category does not distinguish. Colonialism, caste, commons, and the phrase “legality becomes a weapon” are located in this book's conceptual argument, not presented as causal results of the dataset.

The Bengali passage is an explicitly identified lexical reading. “Not found” is a gloss on the source wording; “absent” is contextualized through questions of place and time. No interview, personal incident, field observation, poem, or attributed human voice has been invented. Bengali copyediting remains advisable.

The 2002 archive is removed from the manuscript because no matched historical analysis is used in its argument. It remains a separate research resource. The film material is reduced to one general analogy about the finished surface; it carries no CBFC numerical claims and promises no second empirical case. All prior CBFC reproduction and visual limitations remain recorded in the [September review](repository-review-2026-09-28.md).

The essay speaks from inside “this book.” It does not attribute arguments to Shuddhabrata Sengupta, Labani Jangi, or Sucheta De on the basis of the contributor list: their actual texts have not been read here. It states the substantive issue of contestation—eligible people having to seek restoration of an omitted entry—without inventing a sourced chronology of protests or litigation.
